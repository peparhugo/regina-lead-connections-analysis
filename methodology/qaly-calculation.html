QALY Calculation Methodology

This document provides the complete derivation of Quality-Adjusted Life Year (QALY) estimates for childhood lead exposure from lead service lines in Regina, Saskatchewan. The methodology follows established health economics frameworks and is grounded in peer-reviewed literature.

1. Conceptual Framework

Quality-Adjusted Life Years (QALYs) provide a standardized measure of disease burden that combines quality of life impacts with duration. For childhood lead exposure, QALY losses arise primarily from cognitive impairment (IQ reduction) and associated lifetime consequences including reduced educational attainment, earnings, and increased healthcare utilization.

This analysis follows the approach established by Gould (2009) and subsequently validated by Attina & Trasande (2013), adapted for Canadian cost parameters using CIHI and CADTH guidelines.

2. Core Formula

QALY_realized = Children_affected × Exposure_Years × QALY_per_child_year
Where:
• Children_affected = Lead connections × Children per connection
• Exposure_Years = Cumulative years since 2019 baseline
• QALY_per_child_year = Annual QALY decrement per exposed child

The base case value of 0.201 children (ages 0-14) per lead connection is derived from Census Tract-level population data weighted by the spatial distribution of lead connections. Census tracts with more lead connections contribute proportionally more to the weighted average.

3.2 QALY per Child-Exposure-Year

ScenarioValueDerivation
Base case0.05Mid-point of literature range
Lower bound0.02Conservative estimate (minimal exposure)
Upper bound0.10Higher exposure / vulnerable populations
Literature Basis: The QALY decrement is derived from IQ-to-QALY mappings. Lanphear et al. (2005) established that blood lead levels of 2.4-30 μg/dL result in IQ reductions of 3.9-7.4 points. Each IQ point lost corresponds to approximately 0.01-0.015 QALYs over a lifetime (Gould 2009), distributed across the exposure period.

3.3 Exposure Duration

Exposure is calculated cumulatively from the 2019 baseline (when the City of Regina's lead service line replacement program began) through the analysis date. For the April 2026 analysis, this represents approximately 7 years of cumulative exposure for connections that remain unreplaced.

The cumulative approach accounts for the fact that lead exposure effects compound over time, with early childhood exposure (ages 0-6) having disproportionate impacts on neurodevelopment.

4. Calculation Steps

  1. Spatial join: Match lead connection points to Census Tracts using PostGIS ST_Within
  2. Child density: Calculate children (0-14) per address from CT demographics
  3. Exposure weighting: Apply time-weighted exposure based on years since 2019
  4. QALY computation: Multiply affected children × exposure years × QALY rate
  5. Aggregation: Sum across Census Tracts for total burden
-- Core QALY calculation (simplified)
SELECT
  ct.ct_uid_code,
  lc.connection_count,
  ct.children_0_14 / ct.total_dwellings AS children_per_dwelling,
  lc.connection_count * (ct.children_0_14 / ct.total_dwellings) AS children_affected,
  -- Cumulative exposure: sum of years since 2019
  EXTRACT(YEAR FROM CURRENT_DATE) - 2019 AS exposure_years,
  -- Base QALY rate
  0.05 AS qaly_per_child_year,
  -- Final QALY calculation
  lc.connection_count
    * (ct.children_0_14 / ct.total_dwellings)
    * (EXTRACT(YEAR FROM CURRENT_DATE) - 2019)
    * 0.05 AS qaly_realized
FROM lead_connections_by_ct lc
JOIN census_tracts ct ON lc.ct_uid_code = ct.ct_uid_code;

6. Limitations and Assumptions

Key limitations:
  • Child distribution is estimated from CT averages, not household-level data
  • Exposure duration assumes continuous residence (no migration adjustment)
  • Blood lead levels are estimated from service line presence, not measured
  • Latent health effects beyond IQ (cardiovascular, behavioral) not fully captured

These limitations suggest the estimates are likely conservative. Actual health burden may be higher when accounting for:

  • Additional lead sources (paint, soil, plumbing fixtures)
  • Cumulative multi-source exposure effects
  • Cardiovascular outcomes (Lanphear 2018)
  • Behavioral and attention disorders (Braun 2006)
