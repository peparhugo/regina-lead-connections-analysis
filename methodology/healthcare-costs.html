Regina Lead Analysis / Methodology / Healthcare Costs

Healthcare Cost Model

This document details the cost-of-illness methodology for estimating healthcare and educational costs associated with childhood lead exposure in Regina. The model uses CIHI service costs and accounts for the city's filter distribution program.

1. Methodology: Cost-of-Illness Approach

This analysis uses a cost-of-illness (COI) approach, which sums actual healthcare and educational service costs over the exposure period. This is the standard methodology in health economics for estimating disease burden.

Total_Cost = Exposed_Children x Per_Child_Service_Cost x Duration
Where:
• Exposed_Children = Population adjusted for filter adoption
• Per_Child_Service_Cost = CIHI-derived service costs by phase
• Duration = 18 years (ages 0-18), discounted at 3%
Methodology Note: This replaces an earlier approach that incorrectly multiplied QALYs by willingness-to-pay thresholds. The CADTH $50,000/QALY threshold is for evaluating intervention cost-effectiveness, not for calculating healthcare costs.

Conservative assumption: This calculation gives partial credit for filter adoption. In reality, filters certified to 150 ug/L may provide minimal protection in homes with readings of 500-1,800 ug/L.

6. Testing Data Context

Highest reading recorded 1,801 ug/L 360x limit

Filter certification limit 150 ug/L Exceeded in multiple homes
Filter limitation: Standard Brita filters are certified to reduce lead at concentrations up to 150 ug/L. In homes with readings of 500-1,800 ug/L, filter efficacy is uncertain.

7. Methodology Validation

This cost-of-illness estimate was validated using multi-model consensus testing with GPT-5-mini, GPT-4.1, Claude Sonnet 4, and Claude Opus 4.5. All models converged on per-child NPV estimates within 3% of each other ($96,600 - $99,950).

International comparison: The per-child estimate of ~$100,000 CAD NPV ($73,000 USD) falls within the range reported in US studies: Gould (2009) estimated $10,000-$21,000 per affected child in direct costs, while including productivity losses increases estimates to $50,000-$80,000 per child.
