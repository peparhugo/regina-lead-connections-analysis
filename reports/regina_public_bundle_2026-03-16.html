Bottom line

Regina did not stand still on lead service connections. Council and committee records show a sequence of formal actions from 2021 to 2025 that expanded replacement rules, financing access, filter support, and reporting direction.

Best current reading: this is real incremental policy movement, supported by official records, with sustained public pressure present in the process. What the evidence does not prove is that any single delegation directly caused a later vote.

Human-impact frame: this is not only a policy-process or infrastructure story. The project’s research base also supports a cautious child-development and long-run health framing: lead exposure is linked in the literature to cognitive impacts, ADHD-type burden, and increased later chronic disease risk. That means delay should be judged not only in dollars, but in quality of life and healthy years put at risk.

Health burden beyond dollars

Lead exposure should not be framed only as a financing or engineering issue. The evidence set assembled in this project supports a cautious public-health framing: prolonged exposure is linked in the literature to child-development impacts, behavioural impacts, and increased later-life chronic disease risk.

Technical appendix — child cognitive burden envelope

This appendix converts the project’s child-development concern into a narrow technical burden estimate. Using a conservative mild developmental-burden crosswalk, a low burden-share assumption, and a medium persistence horizon, the child-cognitive lane alone produces a base-case model output of 3.696 YLD-equivalent units under the appendix assumptions.

Interpretation: this is a bounded appendix estimate, not a headline public claim. It uses a proxy child exposure-context denominator, excludes ADHD stacking, excludes adult chronic disease and mortality lanes, and should not be read as a diagnosed-case count or final attributable citywide burden total.
