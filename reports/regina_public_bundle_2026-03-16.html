What changed — and what did not

The current record supports real movement. Financing and reimbursement tools changed. Filter-related support improved. Reporting expectations were reinforced. Outreach and follow-up directions became more explicit. Public and delegate pressure also appears in the official process.

But the same record does not yet support a claim that Regina adopted a fully transformed framework, a formally accelerated citywide completion regime replacing 2036, or a proven delivery reset.

Strongest current reading: incremental movement, not a true policy shift.

What the city framework now appears to be — and what still needs proof

The current record supports a structured city framework: a staged replacement baseline, a conditional private-side obligation rule, an expanded support layer, and a reporting/follow-up architecture.

That matters because it means the framework is real, not symbolic. But the safest current reading is still continuity with bounded improvement rather than a full reset. The 2021 baseline still governs the current read. The 2025 package brought real improvements, but important parts of the framework remain conditional, directional, or unverified in practice.

Key unresolved question: not whether direction exists on paper, but whether delivery and follow-through became strong enough in practice to match the stakes.
