# Regina Lead Program — Conservative Public Health-Burden Table

Date: 2026-03-16

This table is intentionally conservative. It is meant for public communication and operator review, not for claiming a single settled citywide QALY total.

| Burden lane | What the current project supports | What the public can reasonably infer | What we should not claim yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Child cognitive burden | The evidence set links lead exposure in the literature to cognitive harm and IQ-related developmental burden. | Delay may carry a child-development cost, not just a financial one. | A fully quantified Regina attributable IQ-loss total. |
| ADHD / behavioural burden | The evidence set links lead exposure in the literature to ADHD-type and behavioural burden. | Delay may increase the risk of behavioural and school-life burden. | A single citywide attributable ADHD case total without full overlap controls. |
| Adult chronic disease burden | The evidence set supports later-life cardiovascular and kidney risk concern. | Delay may matter for long-run health, not just near-term infrastructure status. | A fully quantified Regina chronic-disease burden attributable to current exposure conditions. |
| Healthy life years | The current framework supports discussing quality of life and healthy years at risk. | Public discussion can move beyond dollars toward preventable lifetime harm. | One headline Regina QALY/DALY number without utility mapping, duration assumptions, and anti-double-counting controls. |

## Public-safe summary line
Lead-service-line delay should be understood in two ways at once: as a systems and budget issue, and as a human burden issue affecting development, health, and quality of life.
