# Investigative Memo — Regina Lead Program Continuity, Pressure, and Policy Movement

Date: 2026-03-12  
Project lane: Regina lead research pivot (publication-oriented)  
Question: Do official records support a continuity narrative with practical pressure and incremental policy movement?

## 1) Method

### 1.1 Scope
- Time window emphasized in evidence chain: 2021–2025 decision nodes, with explicit backward reference to MN19-23 where available in primary records.
- Source type restriction: primary municipal records only (official committee/council minutes and recorded recommendations/votes).

### 1.2 Inclusion criteria
Included only records that satisfy all conditions:
1. Identify a lead-service-connection-related item (CR/EX item).  
2. Contain an explicit recommendation/action text.  
3. Record disposition (carried/lost/adjusted/referral).  
4. Are citable to a stable meeting/minutes artifact.

### 1.3 Validation approach
- Built claim/evidence rows with one claim per minimally sufficient source set.
- Marked each claim as `Accepted` or `Contested/Open` based on direct textual support.
- Separated three layers:  
  - **Formal continuity** (sequence of adopted actions)  
  - **Practical pressure** (delegation/communication presence)  
  - **Policy movement type** (incremental vs structural reset)

## 2) Evidence chain (high-level)

1. **CR21-90 (2021-05-26)** established the main program architecture (15-year timeline, private-side replacement rule, financing/filter/reporting directions, and removal of MN19-23 from outstanding list).  
2. **EX22-23 (2022-03-09)** moved implementation-facing bylaw changes tied to CR21-90.  
3. **CR23-97 (2023-09-13)** continued oversight direction via recurring updates (wording adjustment noted in minutes).  
4. **EX25-82 (2025-09-17) + CR25-115 (2025-09-24)** approved additional affordability/support/reporting/outreach adjustments and follow-up work.

Interpretation: this is a coherent progression of formal municipal actions with additive modifications, not isolated decisions.

## 3) Findings

### Finding A — Formal continuity is supported
The record supports a continuity narrative from initial architecture through subsequent implementation and enhancement decisions.

### Finding B — Practical pressure is observable in process records
Delegations/communications from community actors appear in lead-related items at multiple points. This demonstrates sustained external presence in the formal process.

### Finding C — Movement appears incremental, not transformative
Most approved changes are program-parameter and implementation refinements (eligibility, fees, reimbursement caps, reporting tasks, outreach directives), rather than a full replacement of strategic frame.

## 4) Limits and unresolved points

1. **Causality limit:** Delegation presence cannot be treated as proof of direct causal effect on vote outcomes without additional evidentiary design.
2. **Causality limit (narrowed from prior traceability gap):** The documentary chain for MN19-23 is now sufficient for publication-safe anchoring via the 2019 meeting outline/full packet plus the 2021 CR21-90 minutes that remove MN19-23 from the outstanding list. What remains unresolved is not documentary presence, but whether MN19-23 directly caused the later policy package.
3. **Outcome limit:** This memo assesses policy-process movement, not final exposure outcomes or realized health-risk reduction levels.
4. **Document layer limit:** Minutes capture decisions and procedural outcomes; technical appendices and implementation datasets are needed for performance adjudication.

## 5) What would count as a true policy shift
A “true shift” should be treated as evidenced only if at least 4 of 5 conditions are met in adopted/funded form:

1. **Timeline acceleration:** formally adopted completion horizon materially earlier than current trajectory.  
2. **Equity execution rule:** codified prioritization for highest-risk households/geographies with auditable criteria.  
3. **Delivery standards:** enforceable service standards for outreach, filter distribution, and replacement follow-through.  
4. **Transparent performance:** recurring public reporting with progress and uptake metrics that permit external verification.  
5. **Resource lock-in:** dedicated multi-year budget authority aligned to accelerated goals.

## 6) Practical next research actions

1. Build a decision-to-delivery tracker: each adopted direction mapped to implementation artifact, deadline, and completion status.  
2. Keep the policy-shift scorecard current (0–5 conditions above) as new lead-related items are passed.  
3. If needed for a stronger investigative edition, add the full MN19-23 packet text excerpt directly into the public appendix, but this is now a strengthening step rather than a blocker.
