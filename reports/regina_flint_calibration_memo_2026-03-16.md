# Regina Lead Program — Flint Calibration Memo

Date: 2026-03-16  
Status: Comparator-case calibration note for human-burden modeling

## Purpose
This memo explains how Flint should and should not be used in the Regina burden model.

---

## Core answer
Flint is useful as a **comparator case**, not as a direct burden transplant.

That means Flint can help answer:
- what a severe municipal lead crisis can look like,
- what order-of-magnitude child-risk concern is plausible,
- and how cautious or aggressive the Regina scenario bounds should be.

It should **not** be used to claim:
- Regina had the same realized burden,
- Regina had the same exposure distribution,
- or Regina should inherit Flint’s outcomes one-for-one.

---

## Comparator anchor used here
### 2017 Flint pediatric review
**Pediatric lead exposure and the water crisis in Flint, Michigan**  
DOI: `10.1097/01.jaa.0000511794.60054.eb`

Crossref abstract summary indicates:
- Flint’s water-source change exposed residents to elevated lead levels,
- and the incidence of elevated blood lead levels among children in the affected area nearly doubled after the switch.

This is useful because it gives a clear peer-reviewed signal that a municipal water-system failure can translate into measurable child blood-lead burden.

---

## How Flint is used in the Regina model
### 1) Upper-bound seriousness check
Flint supports the argument that:
- municipal water-related lead failures can produce meaningful child exposure consequences,
- and therefore Regina should not be treated as a purely administrative or pipe-accounting issue.

### 2) Scenario bounding logic
Flint supports the use of:
- a conservative low scenario,
- a more plausible base scenario,
- and a broader upper scenario,
without pretending that all three are equally likely.

### 3) Communication discipline
Flint helps communicate why delay matters, but only if Regina-facing outputs state clearly that:
- Flint is a comparator,
- not an identity claim,
- and not a replacement for Regina-specific surveillance.

---

## What prevents direct transfer from Flint to Regina
Key non-equivalence risks include:
- different water chemistry and corrosion-control history,
- different exposure duration,
- different age mix and household conditions,
- different blood-lead screening intensity,
- different timing of public response,
- different environmental co-exposures,
- different baseline vulnerability and service access.

Because of these differences, Flint should calibrate **sensitivity bounds**, not determine final burden values.

---

## Operational rule for the Regina model
Use Flint as:
- **evidence that child impact can be real and measurable**, and
- **a justification for not choosing unrealistically tiny burden-share assumptions**.

Do not use Flint as:
- the direct numerator for Regina burden,
- the direct prevalence estimate for exposed children,
- or the direct source of disability weights.

---

## Modeling implication
The child-cognitive burden prototype should therefore be interpreted as:
- primarily grounded in Regina proxy-context figures,
- translated through a standardized burden-weight family,
- and bounded with the caution that comparator cases like Flint show why a non-trivial upper scenario must remain on the table.

---

## Bottom line
Flint is methodologically useful for Regina, but only in the right role:

**not copy-paste, but calibration.**
