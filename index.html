CASE FILE: REGINA LEAD SERVICE CONNECTIONS

Compiled April 2026 from public records

In one neighbourhood, 1,380 children share their water with 459 lead pipes.

The city replaces them at the same rate as affluent areas with almost none. On May 26, 2021, council voted 7–4 to keep the pipes in the ground for five more years. We downloaded the city's own data to find out what that decision cost.

19 neighbourhoods 470 years of healthy childhood lost $83.7M burden 4–7 vote
01 The neighbourhood

The crisis is not evenly distributed.

Lead pipes exist in 19 census tracts. The rest of the city has none. The replacement rate across rich and poor tracts is flat — 35–40% — regardless of how many children, how much poverty, or how high the Indigenous population.

02 The vote

The city chose 2036.

On May 26, 2021, Regina City Council considered CR21-90 — the Lead Service Connection Management Program. Councillors Stevens and LeBlanc moved an amendment: complete all replacements by 2031 instead of 2036.

The amendment was defeated 4–7.

Key discrepancy

The city told the Get The Lead Out committee there are ~2,305 connections. Their own GIS shows 2,760 active Pb. The full network trace shows 5,405. Which number is real?

All five layers are publicly accessible via the Regina OpenGIS REST API.

The tracking gap

On January 23, 2024, someone at City Hall entered 2,021 records into the GIS in a single day. Years of backlogged work, uploaded in one sweep. The city tracks this crisis off the side of someone's desk.

QALY & cost model

Track 1 — Individual quality: 0.015 QALY/affected-child/year for 30 years (NPV at 3%). Range: 0.005 (low) to 0.030 (high).

Track 2 — Economic: $24,000 CAD per IQ point lost (Gould 2009, inflation-adjusted) × 2.0 IQ points per child (base case). Already represents lifetime NPV.

Cost breakdown ratios: Earnings 59.9%, Healthcare 18.8%, Education 12.0%, Tax 9.0%, Justice 0.3%. Derived from Gould (2009) with healthcare adjusted for 30-year chronic disease tail.

Coverage correction: ×1.189 applied to geospatial replacement counts (84.1% visibility).
