Regina Lead Service Connections — What Changed, What Hasn’t, and Why It Matters

City records show the lead-service-line problem was on the formal agenda years ago. This project tracks what officials adopted, what changed over time, and where the gap between recognition and delivered protection still matters.

It is a story about infrastructure and delivery — but also about residents, families, child-development risk, and why delay should not be judged only in budget terms.

Health Burden Beyond Dollars

Why faster replacement matters in human terms, not only budget terms.

Lead service line delay is not only a financing or infrastructure problem. The research base assembled for this project permits a cautious human-impact framing: prolonged exposure is linked in the literature to child-development impacts, ADHD-type burden, and increased later chronic disease risk. That means delay should be understood not only in dollars, but also in terms of development, health, and quality of life.

Children
The strongest evidence in the project points to cognitive and behavioural burden in children, including IQ loss and ADHD-related harms.
Current proxy context: 1,540 children 0–14 in the proxy exposure context (inferred proxy, not audited count)
Adults
The evidence base also supports caution around later chronic disease burden, especially cardiovascular and kidney impacts associated with lead exposure.
See existing CVD and CKD effect anchors in the repo evidence set
