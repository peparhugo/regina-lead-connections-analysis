The short version

The public case does not depend on one dramatic claim. It rests on a simpler, stronger read: the issue was known, the program moved in stages, and the consequences of slow replacement matter most where exposure burden is hardest to absorb.

The issue was already in the official record

Council records show the lead-service-line problem had already entered the formal agenda years ago. This is not a story of sudden discovery.

Policy moved, but in incremental packages

The record supports movement through motions, plans, financing changes, outreach changes, and reporting adjustments — not a clean single-turn resolution.

Delay is not just an infrastructure story

The public framing here is deliberately human: families, children, and avoidable exposure burden matter, even when the page keeps the harder numeric burden work in the appendix layer.

Bottom line: the strongest public argument is about recognition, delivery, and consequences — not about dressing a technical archive up as the story itself.

Timeline of the strongest public record points

This section keeps the chronology readable. It is meant for people who want the backbone of the story without opening the full evidentiary package first.

2019

Acceleration enters the formal record

MN19-23 places acceleration of the lead-service-connection program into the official Council record.

2021

The baseline replacement program is adopted

CR21-90 approves a 15-year replacement plan, private-side replacement rules, financing supports, and filter distribution direction.

2022

Implementation mechanics move

Executive Committee and Council continue bylaw and administrative changes tied to the adopted package.

2023

Oversight remains active

Recurring updates and reporting directions keep the program visible in the record.

2025

Another adjustment package lands

Financing, fee, filter reimbursement, outreach, and reporting changes are approved.

Current read

Movement is real, but the delivery story remains the point

The documentary record supports incremental policy movement, sustained attention, and a still-important question about pace, burden, and protection.

Why this matters beyond dollars

The public-facing human-impact framing belongs on the page. The harder technical burden modeling belongs in the support layer unless and until it is ready for stronger public use.

Children carry the sharpest public-risk story

The strongest evidence path in this package concerns child-development burden, including cognitive and behavioural harms associated with lead exposure.

Adults are part of the burden picture too

The wider literature also supports caution about longer-run cardiovascular and kidney burden associated with lead exposure.

Keep the numeric burden work where it belongs

The appendix-grade child-cognitive burden material is included as support, not as the main headline claim on this page.

Interpretation rule: this page supports a cautious human-impact framing. It does not claim a single settled citywide QALY headline.
