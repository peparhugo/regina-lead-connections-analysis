Regina lead data

Five decisions. One slow public health failure.

Scroll through the evidence: how many lead pipes remain, what the health burden costs, who bears it, how council slowed the timeline, and what residents can demand next.

At a glance

Regina still has 2,743 lead pipes. The modeled burden reaches 179 years of healthy life lost and $47.6 million in healthcare costs, concentrated in renter-heavy neighborhoods while replacement remains politically delayed.

01
The backlog

2,743 lead pipes still poison Regina's water. 12 more years.

The city has reduced the total since 2019, but the remaining inventory still stretches across years of daily exposure. At the recent pace, full removal remains a next-decade problem.

The harm

179 years of healthy life lost. $47.6M healthcare costs.

Those numbers turn hidden exposure into public burden: lost healthy life, avoidable care, and irreversible damage absorbed long before the last pipe is replaced.

Environmental inequity

High-renter neighborhoods bear the burden. 82% renters.

Exposure clusters where tenants have the least control over replacement decisions. Renters can drink the risk without owning the line, the property, or the timeline.

The vote

Council rejected 10-year timeline. CR21-90-AMEND defeated 7-4.

This was not just engineering drift. Council had a chance to accelerate removal and chose the slower path, extending the period of exposure into more childhoods.

Amendment outcomeDefeated 7-4
What was lostA faster 10-year target

Tenant actions

Tenants can request a filter and testing directly. Saskatchewan still leaves major disclosure gaps.

Rights summary

  • Saskatchewan does not require landlords to disclose known lead service connections.
  • You can still request a city filter and water testing without landlord permission.
  • If the landlord ignores concerns, contact the Office of Residential Tenancies.
