The bigger picture

Regina had the issue on its agenda early. The accountability question is why the response stayed so gradual.

The record shows early political notice, a real legal framework by 2022, and barrier-reduction changes still happening in 2025. The public question is whether that response became strong enough, fast enough, to count as real protection.

The main finding

If someone only reads one section, it should be this one.

What was known

The issue was already in the formal record by 2019

Council records show that accelerating lead service connection action was already a live issue at City Hall.

What changed

The City later built a real legal framework

By 2022, Regina had enacted bylaw changes covering required replacement conditions, payment structures, and tax-administration rules.

What remains unresolved

The strongest question is why barriers remained for so long

By 2025, the City was still removing the administration fee and broadening eligibility for the longer repayment option.

What changed — and what did not change enough

The easiest way to understand the story is to look at both sides at once.

What changed

From 2022 to 2025, the record shows real movement: legal changes, payment-plan rules, tax handling, and later removal of the admin fee plus broader access to the 10-year repayment path.

What did not change enough

The legal structure still contained meaningful access friction for years, including a $240 administration fee and tighter eligibility rules for the longer repayment option.

The accountability question

Once a city knows the issue is real, how long can the response stay staged and conditional before delay becomes part of the problem?

Quick timeline

The timeline helps show continuity: recognition first, policy movement after, and a still-open delivery question at the end.

2019

Political notice. The issue reached Council, but the acceleration motion was referred for administrative scoping rather than enacted as a binding 2025 mandate.

2022

Legal framework. Recovered bylaw text shows the City had moved the file into law, including required-replacement conditions in some cases and an equalized-payment structure.

2025

Barriers eased later. Recovered bylaw text shows the City removed the administration fee and broadened eligibility for the 10-year repayment option.
