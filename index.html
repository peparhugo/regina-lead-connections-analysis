The rental trap

Tenants carry the risk. Landlords carry almost no obligations.

In the worst-hit neighborhoods, renter shares run from 45% to over 75%. Saskatchewan does not require landlords to test, disclose, or remediate lead service lines. Tenants cannot authorize replacement. 27% of lead-connection addresses had filter coverage in 2024 — meaning nearly three quarters of affected households relied on unfiltered water.

The vote

The previous council chose a slower timeline. The voters replaced them. The slower timeline remains.

All named councillors below served in the 2020–2024 term. None remain on council after the November 2024 election. Right-of-reply letters have been sent to each. But the 2036 deadline they set is still the official plan.

CR21-90 — May 2021

7–4 against finishing by 2031

The amendment for a 10-year timeline failed. Bob Hawkins, John Findura, and Sandra Masters were among the seven who voted to keep the slower schedule.

CR23-97 — 2023

6–3 for reporting — with key NO votes

Even formal reporting requirements faced resistance. Findura, Hawkins, and Masters voted NO.

What you can do

Three actions. All of them start today.

01

Test your water

The City of Regina offers free lead testing kits. If your home was built before 1980, request one. You cannot see, smell, or taste lead in water. Request a test →

02

Contact your councillor

Ask your ward councillor what they are doing to accelerate replacement. Ask Mayor Bachynski whether 2036 is fast enough. Contact info is in the table above. Find your ward →

03

Join Get The Lead Out

The Cathedral Area Community Association leads the campaign for faster replacement. They organized the September 2025 presentation to the Executive Committee that secured the first public address list. Get involved →
