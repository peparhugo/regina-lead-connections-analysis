The harm

The numbers tell one story. Delay converts preventable harm into permanent loss.

Replaced

What has been done

939 connections 25.8% of the original 3,637

Cathedral (277) and North Central (238) saw the most work. But that still leaves 74.2% of all lead service connections in the ground.

Remaining

What is still in the ground

2,698 connections ~1,600 children estimated affected

Every day these pipes stay connected, children drink water that has passed through lead. The city's own plan targets 2036 for full replacement — 10 more years.

Who blocked action

Regina council was not neutral

When faster replacement and basic reporting accountability came to council, several councillors repeatedly landed on the wrong side of the vote.

CR21-90

7–4 against faster replacement

On the amendment pushing a faster 10-year timeline, the vote failed. Bob Hawkins, John Findura, and Sandra Masters were among the councillors who opposed faster action, along with four others.

CR23-97

6–3 on reporting — with key NO votes

Even formal reporting faced resistance. Findura, Hawkins, and Masters voted NO on reporting.

The provincial vacuum

Saskatchewan left municipalities and tenants exposed

Saskatchewan

  • No provincial lead-in-water standard
  • No landlord disclosure requirement
  • No dedicated provincial funding stream

What stronger provinces do

  • Set explicit standards or action thresholds
  • Require clearer testing and disclosure expectations
  • Back local remediation with provincial money

The rental trap

Tenants carry the risk while landlords face almost no lead-specific obligations

In the worst-hit neighborhoods, renter shares run from 45% to more than 75%. Yet Saskatchewan does not require landlords to test for lead, disclose lead service connections, or proactively remediate the risk. For renters, “choice” is mostly fiction: they often do not know the line is lead, cannot authorize replacement themselves, and may not even control plumbing decisions inside the home.

The gap in plain language

  • Landlords are not required to disclose lead service lines to tenants.
  • Tenants cannot force private-side replacement on their own.
  • Filter programs do not fix ownership and disclosure failures.
  • Low filter uptake means the fallback safety net is weak.

Full Methodology & Claim Justification

Every claim on this site is traceable to source data, reproducible queries, and peer-reviewed literature.

Formula: QALY = Σ (Children × Exposure_Weight × QALY_per_Year)

Parameters (base case):

  • QALY per child-exposure-year: 0.05 (range: 0.02-0.10)
  • Children per connection: 0.201 (range: 0.089-0.325)
  • Exposure duration: cumulative since 2019 baseline

Literature basis: Lanphear et al. (2005) pooled analysis of 1,333 children showing 0.46-0.87 IQ points lost per μg/dL blood lead. EPA LCRI (2024) values LSL replacement at $22,000-$50,000 per connection based on IQ and cardiovascular outcomes.

Formula: Cost = QALY × Cost_per_QALY

Parameters:

  • Cost per QALY (base): $13,290 CAD (range: $10,102-$21,281)
  • Source: CIHI Patient Cost Estimator (2024), CADTH Guidelines (2017)
  • Includes: developmental services, special education, healthcare utilization

Validation: Within range of US estimates ($10K-$21K USD) from Gould (2009) and Attina & Trasande (2013).
